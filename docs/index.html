Why OVERSEER?

Grounding, status, and speech support without an app drawer

Deck 01 // The Dial

Live recreation, not a screenshot — telemetry values match the reference photo you sent. Toggle the background: GRID is what ships today; PCB is fully built in the app but not switched on by default.

What's a meter vs. a state

Only the HP arc is a meter — it fills proportionally (65% here) and sits over its own faint always-on track, with a soft glow behind it once overcharge crosses 50%, which is also what turns it gold instead of its usual green. The ACK arc and the FLUX arc are always drawn at their full length; what changes is their color and opacity, not how much of the ring they cover. Offline drops any of the three to dark gray; ambient mode drops everything to flat gray or transparent.

Two backgrounds, one dial

bgMode swaps what renders behind the arcs — a dual-speed drifting grid (two overlaid layers, one at 8s per cycle, one at 3s) or procedurally generated PCB traces with animated signal pulses, a live rainbow shader, and gyroscope-driven parallax on the physical watch.

Currently gated: ENABLE_PCB_LAYOUT = false in the source — PCB mode exists, runs, and is what you're seeing in the toggle above, but it isn't reachable in the shipped app yet.

Curved, not straight

The color ring itself rides right at the edge of the display — Compose draws it with no explicit bounding box, so it defaults to the full canvas. Every label sits nested just inside that ring, not floating beyond it toward the bezel, matching Wear Compose's curvedText. Labels in the upper half of the ring (H2O/HP/MEAL, FLUX) read outward, left-to-right, clockwise. The ACK label sits centered on the bottom of the ring, so it reads the opposite way — right-to-left in path terms, glyphs facing inward — which is the only way text at the bottom of a circle avoids rendering upside-down.

Deck 02 // Zones

No single screen recreates this one — it's the tap/hold behavior of the whole face, mapped out.

Three slices, not three arcs

The gesture zones aren't read off the color arcs at runtime — they're three hardcoded 120° sectors, computed from atan2(dy, dx) + 90 on every tap. But their boundaries land, almost to the degree, on the midpoints of the 6° gaps between the arcs (12, 4, and 8 o'clock) — so the zone you tap lines up with the color you're looking at, even though the code never checks the arc itself.

  • VITALITY.SYS (upper right) — tap launches the app directly.
  • ACK (bottom) — tap does not launch ACK. It opens an on-face control overlay instead — see Deck 3.
  • NEONFLUX (upper left) — tap launches the app directly.

Long-press anywhere in a zone kills that app instead, gated by a confirm screen — see Deck 5.

Two different centers

Tap the middle 35% of the face and you get the Options overlay (Deck 4). Long-press has to land inside a smaller 25% to kill everything — it's checked as a separate radius, not the same boundary as the tap.

The gap in between: a long-press landing between 25% and 35% of the radius falls outside the "kill everything" circle, so it's treated as an outer-zone press — killing whichever app that angle points to, not the tap target directly under your finger.

Deck 03 // ACK Control

A real screen, not a diagram — this is what covers the whole face when you tap the ACK zone. Toggle DECK / TARGET to see both modes; on the watch that same switch happens on long-press.

One overlay, two jobs

DECK mode pages through configured ACK decks. TARGET mode pages through that deck's nine link slots (eight targets plus a CLEAR / disengage slot). Long-press anywhere on the overlay flips between them — there's no button for it, just the same hold gesture reused for a different purpose than it has out on the dial.

Three ways to page

  • Rotary crown / bezel: the primary input — each detent past a small scroll threshold sends NEXT_DECK/PREV_DECK or SET_TARGET:<n> to ACK over a broadcast.
  • Tap left / right: the same two commands, for watches without a usable crown — but only in the top 70% of the screen, so it doesn't compete with the CRYO button below.
  • Everything sends a matching haptic buzz and a short synth tone — confirmation you don't have to look for.

CRYO

A dedicated sleep toggle for ACK itself, independent of DECK/TARGET mode. INIT CRYO puts ACK to sleep; while sleeping, DECK mode shows SLEEPING instead of a deck name and the button reads WAKE SYS in blue instead of red.

Deck 04 // Options

The slider is live — drag it or tap a point on the track.

Two shortcuts

NEURO launches a fourth companion app (com.snakesan.cyberquake) not otherwise surfaced on the dial. CYCLE BG steps the background through the same three modes shown in Deck 1 — grid, PCB, starfield — and writes the choice to SharedPreferences under cached_bg_mode so it survives a restart.

How long a tap keeps the screen on

FULL COLOR HOLD sets how long the display stays in its full-color interactive state after you touch it, before dropping back to ambient — three steps, 20 to 30 seconds in 5 second increments, set by dragging or tapping the track directly.

Label doesn't match the range: the track's own left-edge label reads 5, but the real minimum — MIN_COLOR_HOLD_SECONDS in the source — is 20. Looks like a leftover from an earlier version of the range that never got updated. Reproduced here as-is, mismatch included.

Deck 05 // Emergency Stop

Reached from a long-press — center for everything, a zone for one app. See Deck 2.

The only overlay that won't let you tap past it

Every other overlay on this page dismisses if you tap outside its content — Options included. This one doesn't: the background is a no-op click absorber, so the only way out is ABORT or EXECUTE. It's the one interaction on the whole face that requires an explicit choice.

What EXECUTE actually does

Sends ACTION_KILL_SERVICE as a broadcast — targeted at one package, or with no package set at all for ALL — then flips the matching offline flag (or all three) on the dial state. That's what turns an arc dark gray and adds the OFFLINE label back in Deck 1, immediately, no confirmation round-trip needed.

The label isn't uniform: killing everything shows the deliberately-written SYSTEM. Killing one app shows whatever target.substringAfterLast('.') returns — for VITALITY.SYS that's the raw, lowercase vitalitysys, not a clean display name. Reproduced here exactly as it renders.

Deck 06 // Ambient Mode

Same telemetry as Deck 1, both render states. The jitter in ambient is sped up here — the real device rescales roughly once a minute.

A different watch face, not a dimmed one

Ambient isn't the active dial with the brightness turned down — almost everything renders differently. Stroke width drops from 18dp to 4dp. The grid, PCB, and starfield backgrounds all stop rendering — ambient is always flat black. The three RAM/battery/power gauges disappear entirely, replaced by one plain gray outline circle. Every color collapses to a single gray, including the telemetry arcs' own state colors.

The clock stops being solid

Active mode fills the time in solid color. Ambient renders it as an outline only — a 2dp stroke with no fill — and drops the seconds readout completely. Both are standard OLED burn-in mitigations: less lit pixel area, and a value that doesn't need to repaint every second.

The jitter

On every ambient update — about once a minute, driven by the system, not the app — the whole face gets rescaled to a random 85–100%. Small enough not to read as motion, large enough that no pixel stays lit at exactly the same brightness for hours at a time.

One thing stays white: an OFFLINE label is hardcoded to plain white in ambient mode while everything around it goes gray — the one deliberate exception to the blackout, so a real problem stays legible even in the dimmest state.

A preview that actually matches

The ring here isn't a generic 3-way split — it's drawn with the exact same start angles as the watch face's own arcs (-87°, 33°, 153°), pulled from the same source both sides share. Reassign a wedge and this preview redraws immediately; the watch doesn't hear about it until you push.

Nothing is live until you say so

Every edit here — a function moved to a different wedge, a shortcut's label or color — only changes the phone's local config. The watch keeps running whatever it was last pushed, even if it now disagrees with what's on screen here. PUSH TO WATCH is the only action that changes what you see on your wrist, and it always asks first.

Two ways to fill a wedge

FUNCTION mode assigns one of the three built-ins and keeps its live telemetry — this is the mode every wedge starts in. SHORTCUT mode gives up live data for a plain app launcher, with its own label and a color pulled from a fixed set of eight named neon presets, not a continuous picker — easier to land on exactly, and every option is already legible against the dark theme.

Nothing is empty by accident

Since a function can only live in one wedge, picking one that's already placed elsewhere doesn't just fail or leave a gap — saving swaps it, handing your other wedge whatever this one was just holding. Try selecting VITALITY or FLUX above, then SAVE.
